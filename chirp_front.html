Two black holes, one chirp

A black hole is a region where gravity is so strong that nothing — not even light — escapes. Two of them can orbit as a binary. They radiate gravitational waves (ripples in spacetime), lose energy, spiral closer, orbit faster — and merge. The wave rises in pitch and loudness: a chirp.

how it was built

The recipe

🛰 Data

Real GWOSC O3 strain from H1 and L1, 4 s frames whitened with per-block PSDs; the central 2 s is the window.

💥 Injections

Precessing and aligned-spin black-hole binaries, network SNR 4–30, merger anywhere from 0.6 to 1.8 s.

⚡ Glitches

Blips, chirping whistles, scattered light, sine-Gaussians — in both classes so a glitch never leaks the label.

🧠 Model

1-D ResNet, GroupNorm, avg+max pooling, EMA weights, H1↔L1 swap / sign flip / time-shift augmentation, mixed precision.

📐 Baseline

4000-template bank, per-PSD whitened filter, H1–L1 coincidence, Allen χ² reweighting; same windows, same FAP calibration.

🔎 Checks

Self-tests, fitting-factor diagnostic, and forensics that exposed 1000σ spikes hiding in "clean" noise.

questions

Honest answers

Does it detect real events like GW150914?

Not tested yet. It was evaluated on held-out windows with injected signals in real O3 noise. Real events are the next test.

Why compare with matched filtering?

It is the optimal method for Gaussian noise and the backbone of real searches. A fair baseline is the only way to know whether the CNN adds anything.

What does "FAP 10⁻³" mean?

The threshold is set so only 1 in 1000 noise-only windows is flagged. It is per 2 s window, not a physical rate, and windows overlap.

Are the glitches real?

No — they are synthetic, and the network trained on the same families. Robustness to real glitches is untested.

What are the main limitations?

One training seed; an aligned-spin, 22-mode baseline without a coherent sky search; the matched-filter curve is still preliminary.
